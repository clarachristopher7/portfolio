# Domain Lookup Tool

<p className="hub-sub">When an Internet Threat Protection policy blocked or allowed a website unexpectedly, admins had no direct way to see which part of the policy was responsible.</p>

<dl className="ctx">
  <div><dt>The product</dt><dd>Internet Threat Protection, the part of Cloud Secure Edge that filters web traffic on company devices, blocking malicious sites and the categories an organization chooses to restrict.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Who uses it</dt><dd>IT and security administrators, often mid-incident, with an employee waiting on a blocked site.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>What they’re doing</dt><dd>Diagnosing a policy decision. Every website request passes through several layers of rules: protection against known threats, blocked categories such as gambling, blocked applications and sites, and finally any exceptions.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>My part</dt><dd>I suggested building a domain lookup tool into the policy itself.</dd></div>
</dl>

## The problem

A diagnosis depends on the admin’s own configuration: which traffic their organization routes through its private connection, which sites they have listed as exceptions, and which rules they have set, all applied to one specific website. A general diagram cannot answer that, and tracing it by hand is slow and error-prone while a user waits.

## The constraint

The tool does not evaluate blocking by country or by risk score. A clean result could be misread as proof that the policy was not responsible, so the panel states that limit before the admin runs a lookup.
