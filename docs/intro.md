# About me

My name is Clara, and I've spent my career writing for networking and cloud security startups. That means a lot of explaining things you can't see (traffic, tunnels, encryption) to people who have to make decisions about them.

The world I write about is incredibly abstract, so I like to make it concrete by directly using the-thing-itself. What actually happens when I push a button and a VPN turns on? I start with the fundamentals, the most basic building blocks of the technology, before I get into its more nuanced layers. And that's just the first half of my work.

The second half is building a vivid sense of the person using it. Who are they? What are they trying to do? Does what feels unintuitive to me feel unintuitive to them, too?

You're not supposed to say this these days (the hero of our times is the technical specialist), but I'm a creative at heart. I like to expand outward and synthesize. Writing well means moving past the narrow considerations into the larger ones. You have to exercise judgment, know how to reach people and relate to them, and then give them something that flows meaningfully. In many ways, it's better to see a complex technology with innocent eyes, as an outsider, because that's usually closer to where your user is standing.

Right now I own the Cloud Secure Edge docs at SonicWall. I was the only technical writer at Banyan Security, and I stayed on when SonicWall acquired it. Before that, I wrote docs for Tailscale and technical content for Security Compass.

If you're building something complicated and want people to enjoy using it, [I'd love to hear about it](/contact).

## How I work

<div className="ts-steps">

1. **I learn the product before I write about it.** If I can't set it up myself, I can't explain it to someone who has to.
2. **If a screen needs a doc to explain it, I fix the screen first.** The best help text is the help text nobody needs.
3. **Structure is content.** Where something lives tells readers what it is. Put an install guide under "Learn", and nobody will find it.
4. **Tools report, people decide.** I automate the tedious parts of keeping docs accurate, and I keep the judgment calls.

</div>
